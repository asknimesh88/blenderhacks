When you shop for a blender, most attention goes to the motor and blades. But the **jar** (also called the container or pitcher) affects your day-to-day experience just as much: how heavy it is to lift, whether it stains, how it handles hot soup, and how long it lasts.

Most blenders today come with **plastic jars**, many made from tough, BPA-free copolyester (Tritan is a well-known brand name). Some blenders offer **glass jars**, and a few offer **stainless steel**. Each has real strengths and trade-offs.

This guide compares them side by side and helps you choose based on how you cook.

> **How we researched this:** This comparison is based on the general properties of each material, manufacturer specifications and long-term owner feedback. Always check the specific model's manual for heat limits, dishwasher guidance and care instructions.

## Quick comparison

| | Plastic (e.g. Tritan / BPA-free) | Glass | Stainless steel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | Light | Heavy | Light to medium |
| **Shatter resistance** | Very good | Can break or chip if dropped | Excellent |
| **Staining and odors** | Can stain and hold smells over time | Doesn't stain or hold smells | Doesn't stain; may hold strong smells briefly |
| **Scratches and clouding** | Can scratch and cloud with ice and use | Resists scratching | Resists scratching |
| **Heat** | Check limits; many handle hot liquids when rated | Often good with warm liquids; avoid sudden temperature changes | Handles heat well |
| **See your food?** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Common on** | Most blenders, including high-performance | Some conventional blenders | A few premium and commercial blenders |

## Plastic blender jars

Most modern blenders, including nearly all high-performance models, use plastic jars made from durable, shatter-resistant plastics.

**Pros**
- **Lightweight**, so it's easy to lift, pour and clean.
- **Shatter-resistant**, so it survives drops and bumps.
- **Can be shaped for performance**, with square bottoms and vortex-friendly designs that are harder to make in glass.
- **Widely available**, with replacement jars for most models.

**Cons**
- **Can stain** from turmeric, tomato, beets and berries. See [how to remove stains from a plastic blender jar](/blog/remove-stains-from-plastic-blender-jar/).
- **Can cloud and scratch** over time, especially from ice and frozen fruit. See [how to clean a cloudy blender jar](/blog/how-to-clean-a-cloudy-blender-jar/).
- **Can absorb strong smells** like garlic and protein powder.
- **Heat limits vary.** Check the manual before blending hot liquids.

### What about BPA?

Many blender makers now use **BPA-free** plastics and label them clearly. If this matters to you, look for "BPA-free" in the product specifications. The U.S. [Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/) has published information on BPA in food-contact materials if you'd like to read more about the science and regulation.

## Glass blender jars

Glass jars are most common on conventional countertop blenders.

**Pros**
- **Doesn't stain or hold smells**, great for turmeric, tomato sauces and garlicky dips.
- **Resists scratching and clouding**, so it stays clear for years.
- **Feels sturdy and stable** on the base because of its weight.
- **Easy to see when it's truly clean.**

**Cons**
- **Heavy**, especially when full. Harder to lift and pour.
- **Can break or chip** if dropped or knocked, and broken glass in food is a real safety concern.
- **Sensitive to thermal shock.** Pouring very hot liquid into a cold glass jar, or cold water into a hot one, can cause cracks. Let hot foods cool slightly and avoid sudden temperature changes.
- **Less common on high-performance blenders**, so you may have fewer model choices.

**Safety tip:** Check glass jars regularly for chips or cracks, especially around the base. Replace a chipped or cracked jar immediately.

## Stainless steel blender jars

Stainless steel containers are less common, found mainly on some premium and commercial blenders.

**Pros**
- **Extremely durable** and won't shatter.
- **Doesn't stain**, and stays cold for frozen drinks.
- **Handles heat well.**

**Cons**
- **You can't see inside**, so it's harder to judge texture and when to stop.
- **Usually more expensive** and available on fewer models.
- **Can be louder** with ice.

## Which should you choose?

| If you… | Consider… |
|---|---|
| Make frozen drinks and smoothies daily | **Plastic.** Light, shatter-resistant and shaped for good blending |
| Often blend turmeric, curries, tomato or garlic | **Glass**, which won't stain or hold smells |
| Have kids in the kitchen or tile floors | **Plastic or stainless steel.** Less risk from drops |
| Want a high-performance blender | **Plastic.** Most high-performance models use it |
| Blend hot soups often | Any jar **rated for hot liquids** in the manual; or an [immersion blender](/blog/how-to-blend-hot-soup-safely/) |
| Care most about a jar that looks new for years | **Glass or stainless steel** |

<PullQuote>Plastic wins on weight and toughness; glass wins on stains and smells. Choose the jar that fits what you blend most often.</PullQuote>

## How to make any jar last longer

- **Rinse immediately** after blending staining or sticky foods.
- **Use the self-clean method** instead of abrasive scrubbing. See our [60-second cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-a-blender/).
- **Avoid thermal shock**, especially with glass.
- **Hand-wash plastic jars** if the manual recommends it. Dishwasher heat can speed up clouding.
- **Replace the gasket** when it wears out to prevent leaks. See our [blender gasket guide](/blog/blender-gasket-guide/).

## Can you switch jar types on your blender?

Sometimes. Some manufacturers sell alternative containers, such as a glass jar for a blender that shipped with plastic, or extra containers in different sizes. Only use containers the manufacturer lists as compatible with your exact model. Mixing parts can cause leaks, poor blending or damage. Check your manufacturer's parts pages, for example [Oster](https://www.oster.com/) or [Vitamix](https://www.vitamix.com/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a glass or plastic blender jar better?

Neither is better for everyone. Plastic is lighter and shatter-resistant, and it's used on most high-performance blenders. Glass doesn't stain, cloud or hold smells, but it's heavier and can break.

### Are plastic blender jars safe?

Many blender makers use BPA-free plastics designed for food contact. Look for "BPA-free" in the specifications and follow the manufacturer's guidance on heat and cleaning.

### Can you put hot liquid in a glass blender jar?

Often yes, if the manual allows it, but avoid sudden temperature changes that can crack glass, fill no more than about halfway, and vent steam as your manual directs. See our guide to [blending hot soup safely](/blog/how-to-blend-hot-soup-safely/).

### Why is my plastic blender jar cloudy?

Usually mineral deposits, residue or tiny scratches from ice and frozen fruit. Minerals and residue can be cleaned off; scratches are permanent but cosmetic.

### Do glass blender jars break easily?

They're sturdy in normal use, but they can break or chip if dropped or exposed to sudden temperature changes. Check regularly for chips and replace damaged jars.

## The bottom line

**Plastic jars are light, tough and great for everyday smoothies and frozen drinks. Glass jars resist stains and smells and stay clear, but they're heavy and breakable. Stainless steel is ultra-durable but opaque.** Pick the one that matches what you blend most, and care for it well.
