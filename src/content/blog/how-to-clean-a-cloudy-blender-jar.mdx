You wash your blender jar, dry it, hold it up to the light, and it still looks foggy, dull and cloudy. It looks dirty even when it isn't, and no amount of scrubbing seems to help.

A cloudy blender jar is one of the most common blender complaints, especially with plastic jars. The good news is that **most cloudiness can be removed in an hour or two with things you already have**, like white vinegar and baking soda. The less good news is that some cloudiness is actually permanent surface damage, and knowing the difference saves you a lot of scrubbing.

This guide explains what causes cloudiness, how to test which kind you have, step-by-step cleaning methods, and how to prevent it coming back.

> **Before you start:** Unplug the blender and remove the jar from the base. Never put the motor base in water. Check your manual for which parts are dishwasher-safe and any cleaners the manufacturer says to avoid.

## What makes a blender jar cloudy?

There are four main causes:

| Cause | What it looks like | Removable? |
|---|---|---|
| **Mineral deposits (hard water)** | White, chalky film, often worse near the bottom | Yes, with vinegar |
| **Protein and fat residue** | Greasy or filmy haze, especially after protein shakes, nut butter or dairy | Yes, with degreasing and baking soda |
| **Detergent residue** | Hazy film after dishwasher cycles | Usually yes, with vinegar and rinsing |
| **Etching and scratches** | Fine, frosted scratches, often in a ring around the blade area | No, it's permanent surface damage |

### Mineral deposits

If you have hard water, minerals like calcium and magnesium are left behind when water dries on the jar. Over time they build up into a white, chalky film. Mineral film is the most common cause of cloudiness, and the easiest to fix.

### Protein, fat and food residue

Protein powders, dairy, nut butters and oily ingredients can leave a thin, sticky layer (a common side effect of regular [protein smoothies](/blog/high-protein-smoothies-without-protein-powder/) and [homemade nut butter](/blog/how-to-make-nut-butter-in-a-blender/)) that dish soap and a quick rinse don't always remove. Over time it traps more residue and looks hazy.

### Detergent residue

Some dishwasher detergents, especially with hard water, leave a film on plastic. Too much detergent or a short rinse can make it worse.

### Etching and scratches

Every time ice, frozen fruit, grains or hard ingredients spin around the jar at high speed, they cause tiny scratches in the surface. Hot dishwasher cycles and harsh detergents can also etch plastic. After enough use, these micro-scratches make the jar look **frosted**, especially around the blades where ingredients hit hardest.

**Etching can't be cleaned off**, because it isn't something on the surface. It's damage to the surface itself.

## The quick test: dirt or damage?

1. **Wet the cloudy area.** If it becomes clearer while wet and then turns cloudy again as it dries, it's often **mineral film or residue**.
2. **Rub it with a little white vinegar** on a soft cloth. If the cloudiness fades, it's mineral buildup.
3. **Run a fingernail over it.** If you can feel a fine, rough, frosted texture that doesn't change after cleaning, it's likely **etching or scratches**.

## Method 1: Vinegar soak (for mineral film)

This removes hard-water deposits and detergent film from most jars.

1. **Fill the jar about halfway** with equal parts **white vinegar and warm water**. If the film is higher up, fill higher.
2. **Let it soak for 1–4 hours.** Heavy buildup may need longer, even overnight.
3. **Blend briefly** for 30 seconds to swish the solution around the jar (lid on, starting on low).
4. **Pour it out** and scrub gently with a soft sponge or bottle brush.
5. **Wash with warm soapy water** and rinse well to remove the vinegar smell.
6. **Dry immediately** with a lint-free cloth, or air-dry upside down.

**Tip:** For the outside of the jar, wrap a cloth soaked in vinegar around the cloudy area and leave it for 30 minutes.

## Method 2: Baking soda paste (for residue and film)

Baking soda is a gentle abrasive that lifts stubborn residue without scratching most surfaces.

1. **Mix 2–3 tablespoons of baking soda** with a little water to make a paste.
2. **Spread it over the cloudy areas** with a soft sponge or cloth.
3. **Rub gently** in small circles. Don't scrub hard or use abrasive pads, which can scratch plastic.
4. **Let it sit** for 10–15 minutes on stubborn spots.
5. **Rinse thoroughly**, then wash with warm soapy water.

**For greasy protein residue:** Before the paste, blend warm water with a few drops of dish soap for 30–60 seconds using our [60-second self-clean method](/blog/how-to-clean-a-blender/), then use the baking soda paste.

## Method 3: Vinegar and baking soda combination (for heavy buildup)

For jars with both mineral film and residue:

1. Do the **vinegar soak** first (Method 1) to dissolve minerals.
2. Pour out the vinegar and rinse.
3. Use the **baking soda paste** (Method 2) to lift remaining residue.
4. Wash, rinse and dry thoroughly.

**Don't mix vinegar and baking soda together in a closed jar with the lid on.** The reaction releases gas and can push the lid off. Use them one after the other, with a rinse in between.

## What not to use

- **Abrasive scouring pads or steel wool.** They scratch plastic and make cloudiness worse.
- **Bleach on a regular basis.** It can weaken some plastics and isn't needed for cloudiness. Check your manual before using any bleach-based cleaner.
- **Harsh chemical cleaners.** Stick to food-safe cleaners on anything that touches food.
- **Mixing cleaning chemicals.** Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia. It can produce dangerous fumes. See the [EPA's guidance on safer cleaning and disinfection](https://www.epa.gov/) for general safety advice.
- **Boiling water in plastic jars**, unless your manual says it's safe. Very hot water can warp or crack some plastics.

<PullQuote>Vinegar fixes minerals, baking soda fixes residue, but nothing fixes etching. That's damage, not dirt.</PullQuote>

## What if the cloudiness is permanent?

If you've tried the methods above and the jar is still frosted, especially around the blades, it's etched. The good news:

- **It's cosmetic.** Etching doesn't affect how the blender works.
- **Keep it clean.** Scratched surfaces can trap residue, so clean promptly after each use.
- **Replace the jar if it's cracked**, deeply scratched, or constantly smells. Many manufacturers, including [Vitamix](https://www.vitamix.com/) and [Ninja](https://www.ninjakitchen.com/), sell replacement jars for specific models.
- **Consider a glass or stainless steel jar** if your blender offers one, as they don't etch the same way. Glass is heavier and breakable, though, and isn't available for every blender.

## How to prevent a cloudy blender jar

1. **Clean right after use.** Don't let smoothies, protein shakes or nut butter dry on.
2. **Use the self-clean method daily:** warm water, a drop of dish soap, 30–60 seconds of blending, then a rinse.
3. **Dry the jar immediately** with a lint-free cloth to prevent water spots, especially if you have hard water.
4. **Hand-wash if your manual recommends it.** Dishwasher heat and detergent speed up clouding and etching on many plastic jars.
5. **Do a monthly vinegar soak** if you have hard water.
6. **Avoid thermal shock.** Don't pour very hot liquid into a cold jar, or ice water into a hot one. It stresses the plastic and can lead to cracks.
7. **Use a soft bottle brush**, not scouring pads.

Dealing with color rather than haze? Orange, red or purple tints from turmeric, tomato or berries need a different approach. See [how to remove stains from a plastic blender jar](/blog/remove-stains-from-plastic-blender-jar/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my blender jar cloudy after washing?

Usually because of hard-water mineral deposits, protein or fat residue, or dishwasher detergent film. If the cloudiness has a frosted, rough feel that doesn't change with cleaning, the jar is etched from scratches, which is permanent.

### How do I get the cloudiness out of a plastic blender?

Soak the jar in equal parts white vinegar and warm water for 1–4 hours, then scrub gently with a baking soda paste and rinse well. Dry immediately to prevent new water spots.

### Can I put vinegar in my blender?

Yes. A vinegar and warm water soak, and a short blend to swish it around, is a common way to remove mineral film. Rinse well afterward. Check your manual for any cleaning restrictions.

### Is a cloudy blender jar safe to use?

Generally yes, if it's clean. Cloudiness from minerals or etching is mostly cosmetic. Replace the jar if it's cracked, deeply damaged, or keeps holding smells.

### How do I stop my blender from getting cloudy?

Clean it straight after every use, dry it immediately, hand-wash if the manual recommends it, avoid abrasive pads, and do a vinegar soak regularly if you have hard water.

## The bottom line

Most cloudy blender jars are covered in **mineral film or residue** that a vinegar soak and a gentle baking soda scrub will remove. If the jar still looks frosted, especially around the blades, it's **etched**. That's cosmetic, permanent, and nothing to worry about as long as the jar is clean and intact.

Keep it clear by cleaning straight after use and drying it immediately. For everyday cleaning, a quick soapy self-clean right after use is enough.
