There are few kitchen moments more annoying than lifting your blender jar and finding a sticky puddle of smoothie on the counter, or worse, dripping into the motor base.

A leaking blender is almost always fixable, and usually cheaply. In most cases the culprit is a small rubber ring, a part that wasn't screwed on tightly enough, or a seal that has simply worn out after years of use.

This guide helps you find exactly **where** the leak starts, explains the six most common causes, and walks you through each fix. Most take less than five minutes.

> **Safety first:** Always **unplug the blender** before inspecting or taking anything apart. Blades are sharp, so handle blade assemblies by their base, not the blades. If liquid has got into the motor base, **don't use the blender** until it's completely dry (see the section on liquid in the base below).

## Step 1: Find where the leak is coming from

"Leaking from the bottom" can mean a few different things, and each points to a different cause. Here's a simple test:

1. Unplug the blender and put the jar on a dry paper towel, off the base.
2. Fill it about one-third with water.
3. Wait two to three minutes **without** running it.
4. Check the paper towel, then run the empty-ish jar briefly on the base (with the lid on) over a dry towel and check again.

| What you see | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Leaks while standing, before you blend | Missing, twisted or damaged gasket, or loose collar (causes 1–2) |
| Leaks only while blending | Overfilling, worn gasket, or loose collar under pressure (causes 1–4) |
| Drips from the center, directly under the blades | Worn blade seal or bearing (cause 5) |
| A wet line along the side or bottom of the jar | Cracked jar (cause 6) |
| Liquid appears around the top, running down the outside | Lid not sealed, or jar overfilled (cause 3) |

Now match what you found to the causes below.

## 1. The gasket is missing, twisted or seated incorrectly

On many conventional and personal blenders, the blade assembly screws into a threaded collar at the bottom of the jar, and a **rubber gasket** (sealing ring) sits between them. That little ring is the only thing stopping liquid escaping.

It's very common to:

- Forget to put the gasket back after cleaning
- Put it back twisted, folded or off-center
- Lose it down the drain or the back of a drawer

**How to fix it:**

1. Unscrew the collar and remove the blade assembly (carefully).
2. Check that the gasket is present, lying **flat and evenly** in its groove, with no twists or folds.
3. Reassemble and tighten the collar firmly by hand.
4. Do the water test again.

If your blender's blades are built into the jar (common on many high-performance blenders), there's no removable gasket like this, so skip to causes 4–6.

## 2. The collar or blade assembly isn't tight enough

Even with a perfect gasket, a collar that's only half screwed on won't seal. It may look fine and then leak as soon as the blender's vibration and pressure build up.

It's also possible to cross-thread the collar, which means screwing it on at a slight angle so it feels tight but isn't sealing evenly.

**How to fix it:**

- Unscrew the collar completely and start again.
- Line it up straight and turn it gently until you feel the threads catch smoothly, then tighten firmly by hand.
- Don't overtighten with tools. That can crack the plastic or crush the gasket.
- On personal blenders, make sure the blade base is screwed onto the cup straight and fully tightened before you flip it onto the motor.

## 3. The jar is overfilled or the lid isn't sealed

Liquid can escape from the top as well as the bottom, and then run down the outside of the jar so it *looks* like a bottom leak.

When a blender runs, the contents are thrown upward and outward into a vortex. If the jar is filled to the brim, or the lid isn't pressed down fully, liquid gets forced out and runs down the jar.

**How to fix it:**

- **Stay below the max fill line.** For thin liquids that splash, stay well below it.
- **Press the lid down firmly** until it's fully seated, and make sure the lid plug (the small center cap on many lids) is locked in place.
- **Start on low speed** and increase gradually, instead of jumping straight to high.
- For big batches, **blend in two rounds.**

> **Hot liquids need extra care.** Hot liquids expand and create steam pressure that can push the lid off and cause serious burns. Only blend hot liquids in a blender your manual says is suitable, never overfill, start on the lowest speed, and follow the manufacturer's instructions exactly. For hot soups, an immersion blender used directly in the pot is often the safer choice.

## 4. The gasket is worn, stretched or cracked

Rubber doesn't last forever. After hundreds of washes, especially in a hot dishwasher, gaskets can harden, flatten, stretch, crack or lose their shape. A worn gasket may seal fine when the jar is still, then leak under the vibration of blending.

**How to tell:**

- Remove the gasket and inspect it closely.
- Look for cracks, nicks, flattened areas or a ring that seems loose or wavy in its groove.
- Compare its thickness all the way around. Worn spots are often thinner.

**How to fix it:**

- **Replace the gasket.** They're usually inexpensive, and many brands sell them for specific models. Search your model number plus "gasket" or "sealing ring", or check the manufacturer's website. [Ninja](https://www.ninjakitchen.com/), [Oster](https://www.oster.com/) and [Hamilton Beach](https://www.hamiltonbeach.com/) all sell parts and publish manuals online.
- Buy a gasket that's listed as compatible with your exact model. A "nearly right" size won't seal.
- To make the new one last longer, follow your manual's cleaning instructions and avoid harsh scrubbing. Our [blender cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-a-blender/) explains how to clean the gasket without damaging it.

## 5. The blade seal or bearing is worn (drips from the center)

If the leak comes from the **very center** of the underside, directly under the blades, the problem is the seal around the blade shaft. Inside the blade assembly, a bearing lets the blades spin, and a seal keeps liquid from leaking along the shaft.

When that seal wears, liquid seeps through slowly, often only while blending. It's usually accompanied by other signs of a tired blade assembly:

- The blade feels gritty, stiff or loose when turned by hand (unplugged, carefully)
- Squealing or grinding noises while running
- Sometimes a hot or burning smell (see [why a blender smells like burning](/blog/blender-smells-like-burning/))

**Why it happens:** Soaking the blade assembly for long periods or putting it in the dishwasher when the manual says not to can wash lubricant out of the bearing. Age and heavy use wear it too.

**How to fix it:**

- **Replace the blade assembly.** For many models, and for most high-performance blenders with removable blade assemblies, you can buy one separately.
- If the blades are permanently built into the jar, you'll need a **replacement jar**.
- Don't try to "pack" the seal with grease or glue. It won't hold and may contaminate food.

## 6. The jar is cracked

Plastic jars can develop hairline cracks from dropping, thermal shock (like cold water in a jar that just came out of a hot dishwasher, or hot liquid in a cold jar), or simply age. Glass jars can chip or crack at the base.

Cracks are often almost invisible until the jar is full.

**How to tell:**

- Fill the jar with water and look for a wet line on a dry paper towel.
- Hold the empty jar up to a light and look carefully around the base and blade area.
- Run a fingernail gently over any suspicious lines.

**How to fix it:**

- **Replace the jar.** Don't try to glue a blender jar. The forces, vibration and contact with food make it unsafe and unreliable.
- Many manufacturers sell replacement jars for specific models. Check that it fits your motor base exactly.

## What to do if liquid got into the motor base

If liquid has dripped into the motor base, act carefully:

1. **Unplug the blender immediately.** Don't switch it on to "test" it.
2. Wipe up any visible liquid on the outside with a dry cloth.
3. Turn the base upside down gently over a towel to let any liquid drain out of the drive socket area.
4. Leave it unplugged in a warm, dry place for **at least 24–48 hours** so it dries completely.
5. Never put the motor base in water or in the dishwasher.
6. If you see corrosion, smell burning, or it doesn't work properly afterward, **stop using it** and contact the manufacturer. It's also worth checking the [Consumer Product Safety Commission recalls database](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls) (US) to see whether your model has a known issue.

For sticky residue around the drive socket, a barely damp cloth or a cotton swab works well. Just make sure everything is completely dry before plugging it back in.

## How to stop your blender leaking again

1. **Check the gasket every time** you reassemble after cleaning: present, flat, untwisted.
2. **Tighten the collar straight and firmly by hand.**
3. **Respect the max fill line**, and stay lower for thin liquids.
4. **Seat the lid fully** and start on low speed.
5. **Clean the blade assembly the way your manual says**, and don't soak it unless the manual says you can.
6. **Avoid thermal shock:** let a hot jar cool before adding cold ingredients, and vice versa.
7. **Replace the gasket every so often** if it looks tired. It's a cheap part that protects your motor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my blender leaking from the bottom all of a sudden?

The most common reasons for a sudden leak are a gasket that was left out, twisted or put back unevenly after cleaning, or a collar that isn't fully tightened. Check those first. If it leaks from the center under the blades, the blade seal has probably worn out.

### Can I use my blender if it's leaking?

It's best not to. Leaking liquid can reach the motor base and cause electrical damage. Fix the cause first. If liquid has already reached the base, unplug it and let it dry completely before trying again.

### Can I use a different brand's gasket?

Only if it's listed as compatible with your exact model. Gaskets vary in diameter and thickness, and one that doesn't fit properly won't seal.

### Does putting the blade assembly in the dishwasher cause leaks?

It can, on models where the manual advises against it. Heat and detergent can wash out bearing lubricant and wear the seal faster. Check your manual. Many recommend hand-washing or the quick "self-clean" method instead.

### How do I know if my blender jar is cracked?

Fill it with water and stand it on a dry paper towel for a few minutes. A wet line or spot points to a crack. You can also hold the empty jar up to a light and inspect the base carefully.

## The bottom line

Start with the easy checks: is the gasket there, flat and untwisted, and is the collar tight? That fixes most leaks. If the leak comes from the center under the blades, the blade assembly's seal is worn and needs replacing. A cracked jar needs a new jar. Whatever the cause, keep liquid out of the motor base, and never run a blender that's wet inside.
