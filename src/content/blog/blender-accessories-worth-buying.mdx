Your blender does the heavy lifting, but a few inexpensive accessories can make it noticeably easier to use, faster to clean and more versatile. Others are gimmicks that end up in the back of a drawer.

This guide covers **10 blender accessories that are genuinely useful**, what each one is for, and **3 you can probably skip**.

> **How we chose:** These picks are based on common blender tasks, the problems readers ask us about most (stalling, stains, leaks, cleanup) and long-term owner feedback. We haven't tested every product. Always check that accessories like tampers, jars and blade assemblies are listed as compatible with your exact blender model.

## 1. A tamper (if your blender supports one)

**What it does:** Pushes thick, frozen ingredients into the blades while the blender runs.

**Why it's worth it:** It's the single biggest upgrade for smoothie bowls, nut butters and frozen desserts. No more stopping, unplugging and stirring. See [how to make a thick smoothie bowl](/blog/how-to-make-a-smoothie-bowl/).

**Important:** Only use a tamper designed for your blender and lid. Never use a spoon or spatula in a running blender.

## 2. A narrow blender spatula

**What it does:** Reaches down around the blades to scrape out every last bit.

**Why it's worth it:** Thick smoothies, nut butter and dips cling to the jar. A narrow, flexible spatula gets it out and saves waste.

## 3. A long-handled cleaning brush

**What it does:** Scrubs the bottom of tall jars and around blades without putting your fingers near them.

**Why it's worth it:** Safer and faster cleanup, especially for dried-on residue. Pair it with our [60-second cleaning method](/blog/how-to-clean-a-blender/).

## 4. Spare gaskets

**What it does:** Replaces the rubber sealing ring under the blade assembly.

**Why it's worth it:** Gaskets wear out, stretch and can grow mold. A spare means no downtime when yours needs replacing. See our [blender gasket guide](/blog/blender-gasket-guide/).

## 5. An extra or smaller jar

**What it does:** Gives you a second container, often in a smaller size.

**Why it's worth it:** A smaller jar handles small batches (dressings, baby food, single smoothies) far better than a big pitcher, because the blades reach the food. A second jar also means you can blend back to back without washing in between. Only buy jars listed as compatible with your model.

## 6. To-go cups or bottles

**What it does:** Lets you blend and go, especially with personal blenders.

**Why it's worth it:** Great for commuters and post-workout shakes. Look for leak-proof lids and insulated options to keep smoothies cold.

## 7. Silicone ice cube trays with lids

**What it does:** Freezes coffee, juice, milk or smoothie leftovers in small cubes.

**Why it's worth it:** Coffee cubes make frozen coffee drinks that don't water down. Small cubes are easier on blenders than big ice blocks. See [frozen coffee drinks in a blender](/blog/frozen-coffee-drinks-in-a-blender/).

## 8. Reusable freezer bags

**What it does:** Holds pre-portioned smoothie ingredients.

**Why it's worth it:** Freezer packs turn smoothies into a two-minute breakfast. See our [smoothie freezer pack guide](/blog/smoothie-freezer-packs/).

## 9. A nut milk bag

**What it does:** Strains homemade almond milk, oat milk and cold brew.

## 10. A silicone mat for under the base

**What it does:** Absorbs vibration and protects your countertop.

**Why it's worth it:** A thin mat makes blenders noticeably quieter. Just don't block the air vents. See [how to make a blender quieter](/blog/how-to-make-a-blender-quieter/).

## 3 accessories you can probably skip

### 1. "Blade sharpening" kits and tricks

Most blender blades are designed to work by speed, not sharpness, and many manufacturers recommend replacing the blade assembly rather than sharpening it. See [can you sharpen blender blades?](/blog/can-you-sharpen-blender-blades/).

### 2. Universal "fits all" blade assemblies and jars

Blender parts are model-specific. "Universal" parts often fit poorly, leak or wobble. Buy parts listed as compatible with your exact model, ideally from the manufacturer.

### 3. Single-purpose gadgets you won't use

Spiralizer attachments, special "smoothie bowl" presses and novelty lids often get used once. If you can already do the job with a knife, a spatula or your existing blender, skip it.

## How to look after your accessories

- **Wash tampers, spatulas and brushes** after each use, and let them dry fully before storing.
- **Air out silicone trays and bags** with the lids open. Silicone can hold smells if stored damp.
- **Rinse nut milk bags straight away**, turn them inside out and hang them to dry.
- **Store spare gaskets** in a sealed bag away from heat and sunlight, so the rubber doesn't dry out.
- **Replace worn items**: frayed brushes, cracked cups and stretched gaskets don't do their job.

## How to check an accessory is compatible

1. **Find your blender's model number**, usually on a sticker under the motor base.
2. **Check the manufacturer's accessories page**, for example [Vitamix](https://www.vitamix.com/), [Ninja](https://www.ninjakitchen.com/) or [NutriBullet](https://www.nutribullet.com/).
3. **Match the model number exactly** on third-party listings.
4. **Check the manual** for any accessories that could affect your warranty.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most useful blender accessory?

For thick smoothies, smoothie bowls and nut butters, a tamper designed for your blender makes the biggest difference. For everyday use, a narrow spatula and a long-handled cleaning brush are the most useful.

### Can I use a spoon instead of a tamper?

Never while the blender is running. Stop the blender, unplug it, stir with a spoon or spatula, then blend again. Only a tamper designed for your blender and lid should be used while it runs.

### Are universal blender jars safe to use?

It's best to avoid them. Blender jars, blades and couplings are model-specific. Parts that don't fit exactly can leak, wobble or damage your blender.

### Do I need a smaller jar for my blender?

If you often blend small amounts like dressings, baby food or single smoothies, a smaller compatible jar blends them much better than a large pitcher.

### How often should I replace my blender gasket?

Many people replace it about once a year with daily use, or sooner if it's cracked, stretched, stiff or moldy.

## The bottom line

A few inexpensive accessories make a big difference: **a tamper for thick blends, a narrow spatula, a long-handled brush, spare gaskets and a smaller jar** top the list. Skip "sharpening" kits and universal parts, and always check compatibility with your exact model.
