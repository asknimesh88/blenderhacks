"Nice cream" is one of those kitchen tricks that sounds too good to be true. You blend frozen bananas, and after a minute or two they transform into something that looks and tastes remarkably like soft-serve ice cream. There's no ice cream maker, no churning, and, if you want, no added sugar at all.

It's also one of the **most demanding jobs you can give a blender**, because you're blending frozen fruit with very little liquid. Get the technique right and it's creamy and thick. Get it wrong and you'll have either a runny smoothie or a stuck blender with the motor roaring and nothing moving.

This guide covers how to prepare bananas, step-by-step blending, which blenders handle it best, eight flavor ideas, a banana-free version, and troubleshooting.

## What is nice cream?

Nice cream is a frozen dessert made mainly from **frozen ripe bananas**. Bananas are naturally sweet and low in water compared with many fruits, and they contain pectin and starch that give a creamy, ice-cream-like texture when blended frozen.

| | Nice cream | Regular ice cream |
|---|---|---|
| **Main ingredient** | Frozen bananas | Cream, milk and sugar |
| **Added sugar** | Optional | Usually yes |
| **Dairy** | Optional | Yes |
| **Equipment** | Blender or food processor | Ice cream maker, or no-churn method |
| **Best eaten** | Immediately (soft serve) or after 1–2 hours in the freezer (scoopable) | Anytime |

## Step 1: Prepare the bananas

This is the most important step. The riper and better-prepared your bananas, the better your nice cream.

1. **Use very ripe bananas** with plenty of brown spots. They're sweeter, creamier and less starchy-tasting. Under-ripe bananas taste bland and chalky.
2. **Peel them before freezing.** Frozen banana peel is very hard to remove.
3. **Slice into coins** about ½ inch (1 cm) thick. Small, even pieces blend far more easily than whole or halved bananas.
4. **Freeze in a single layer** on a lined tray for 2 hours or more, so the slices don't freeze into one solid block.
5. **Transfer to a freezer bag** once frozen. They keep for weeks, so freeze bananas whenever they get too ripe to eat.

**Plan on 2–3 bananas** for two servings.

## Step 2: Blend it

### Which blender works best?

| Blender | Nice cream? |
|---|---|
| **High-performance with tamper** | Best: thick, creamy soft serve in about a minute |
| **Food processor** | Very good: slower, needs scraping, very reliable |
| **Full-size conventional** | Possible with more liquid and frequent stopping |
| **Personal / bullet** | Difficult: usually ends up closer to a thick smoothie |

### How to blend

1. **Let the frozen banana slices sit for 3–5 minutes** at room temperature. This takes the edge off the hardness and is easier on your blender.
2. **Add a small splash of liquid first**, about 2–3 tablespoons of milk or plant milk for 2–3 bananas. Keep it minimal. More liquid makes it easier to blend but softer.
3. **Add the frozen banana slices** on top.
4. **Start on low** and increase gradually to medium-high. Starting slow helps avoid air pockets.
5. **Use the tamper constantly** (high-performance blenders), pressing the bananas into the blades.
6. **Stop as soon as it looks like soft serve**: thick, smooth and swirly, usually after 30–90 seconds. Over-blending warms it up and turns it runny.

**No tamper?** Stop, unplug, scrape down the sides with a spatula, and restart. Repeat as needed. **Never** use a spoon or spatula while the blender is running. If the blades are spinning freely under a dome of frozen banana, see [why your blender won't blend](/blog/blender-not-blending/).

**Protect your motor:** Blending frozen fruit with little liquid is hard work. If the base gets hot or you smell something warm, stop and let it rest. See [why a blender smells like burning](/blog/blender-smells-like-burning/).

## Step 3: Serve or freeze

- **Soft serve:** Eat it immediately. It has the texture of soft-serve ice cream.
- **Scoopable:** Transfer to a container, smooth the top, and freeze for **1–2 hours**. After much longer it freezes rock-hard. Let it soften for 10–15 minutes before scooping.

## 8 nice cream flavors

Each is for 2–3 frozen bananas. Add mix-ins to the blender with the bananas unless noted.

1. **Chocolate:** 2 tablespoons cocoa powder and a pinch of salt.
2. **Peanut butter chocolate:** 1 tablespoon cocoa powder and 2 tablespoons peanut butter. (Make your own with our [nut butter guide](/blog/how-to-make-nut-butter-in-a-blender/).)
3. **Strawberry:** 1 cup frozen strawberries (add a little extra milk).
4. **Mango:** 1 cup frozen mango chunks and a squeeze of lime.
5. **Vanilla cinnamon:** 1 teaspoon vanilla extract and ½ teaspoon cinnamon.
6. **Mint chip:** A few fresh mint leaves (or a drop of mint extract) and a handful of spinach for color. Stir in dark chocolate chips after blending.
7. **Coffee:** 1–2 teaspoons instant espresso powder, or a few [coffee ice cubes](/blog/frozen-coffee-drinks-in-a-blender/).
8. **Cookie dough:** 1 tablespoon almond butter, vanilla and a pinch of salt. Fold in chopped cookies or chocolate chips after blending.

**Mix-ins and toppings:** Stir chunky add-ins (chocolate chips, nuts, cookie pieces) in by hand after blending so they stay chunky.

## Not a banana fan? Try this banana-free version

Frozen mango makes an excellent banana-free "nice cream", or really a sorbet-style soft serve:

- **2 cups frozen mango chunks**
- **2–3 tablespoons Greek yogurt or coconut yogurt** (for creaminess)
- **1–2 teaspoons honey or maple syrup** (optional)
- A squeeze of lime

Blend exactly as above, using a tamper and minimal liquid. For more banana swaps, see our guide to [smoothies without banana](/blog/smoothie-without-banana/).

## Troubleshooting nice cream

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Runny, like a smoothie | Too much liquid, or over-blended | Use less liquid; stop as soon as it's thick; freeze 30–60 min |
| Blades spin, nothing moves | Air pocket; banana pieces too big or too frozen | Use the tamper; let slices soften 3–5 min; scrape down |
| Grainy or icy texture | Bananas not ripe enough, or ice crystals | Use very ripe bananas; store them airtight in the freezer |
| Tastes bland | Under-ripe bananas | Use bananas with lots of brown spots; add vanilla or a pinch of salt |
| Frozen rock-hard | Left in the freezer too long | Soften 10–15 min before scooping, or re-blend briefly |
| Motor hot or smells warm | Overworking the blender | Stop and rest it; add a splash of liquid; blend in shorter bursts |

## A note on nutrition

Nice cream made only from bananas has no added sugar or fat, though bananas naturally contain sugar. Mix-ins like nut butter, chocolate and sweeteners add calories. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can you make nice cream in a regular blender?

Yes, but it's harder. Use small, slightly softened banana slices, a little more liquid, and stop frequently to scrape down the sides. A high-performance blender with a tamper or a food processor makes it much easier.

### Why is my nice cream runny?

Usually there's too much liquid, or it was blended too long and warmed up. Use only a tablespoon or two of liquid, stop as soon as it looks like soft serve, and freeze it for 30–60 minutes if it's too soft.

### Do bananas need to be ripe for nice cream?

Yes. Very ripe bananas with brown spots are sweeter and creamier. Under-ripe bananas make bland, starchy nice cream.

### Can I make nice cream without bananas?

Yes. Frozen mango with a little Greek or coconut yogurt makes a creamy banana-free version. Frozen berries and pineapple work too, though the texture is more like sorbet.

### How long does nice cream last in the freezer?

It's best eaten fresh or after 1–2 hours in the freezer. Stored longer, it freezes very hard, so let it soften for 10–15 minutes before scooping, or re-blend it briefly.

## The bottom line

Nice cream is an easy, healthy frozen treat once you've got the technique down. **Use very ripe bananas, slice them before freezing, add only a splash of liquid, and use the tamper.** Stop blending as soon as it looks like soft serve, and eat it right away or freeze briefly for scoops.
