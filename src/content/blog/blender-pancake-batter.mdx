If you make pancakes the traditional way, you know the routine: one bowl for dry ingredients, another for wet, a whisk, a ladle, and a trail of drips across the counter.

A blender simplifies all of that. Everything goes into one jar, you blend for a few seconds, and you pour the batter straight onto the pan through the spout. **No lumps, no extra bowls, and cleanup takes about a minute.**

This guide covers why blender batter works so well, recipes for fluffy pancakes, thin crêpes and oat pancakes, the mistakes that make pancakes tough or flat, and how to prep batter ahead.

## Why make pancake batter in a blender?

- **No lumps.** The blender breaks up every pocket of flour in seconds.
- **One container.** Measure, mix and pour from the same jar.
- **Clean pouring.** The jar's spout lets you pour neat, even circles without a ladle.
- **Great for crêpes.** Crêpe batter needs to be completely smooth and thin, which is exactly what blenders do best.
- **Blend in extras.** Whole oats, bananas and even cottage cheese blend straight into the batter.

## The golden rule: don't over-blend pancake batter

This is the most important tip in this guide. When flour is mixed with liquid, it starts to develop **gluten**, the protein network that gives bread its chewy structure. A little is fine, but too much makes pancakes **tough, rubbery and flat**.

Blenders are powerful, so it's easy to overdo it:

- **Fluffy pancakes:** Blend for just **5–15 seconds** on low, until the flour disappears. A few small lumps are fine.
- **Crêpes:** Blend longer (**20–30 seconds**) for a completely smooth, thin batter, then **rest it** so the gluten relaxes.

## How to load the blender

Follow the same principle as our [smoothie layering order](/blog/smoothie-layering-order/): **liquids first, dry ingredients on top.**

1. **Wet ingredients first:** milk, eggs, melted butter or oil, vanilla.
2. **Dry ingredients on top:** flour, sugar, baking powder, salt.
3. **Blend on low** until just combined.

Adding liquids first stops flour getting stuck under the blades or packed into the corners of the jar.

## Recipe 1: Fluffy blender pancakes

Makes about 10–12 medium pancakes.

**Ingredients**

- 1¼ cups milk
- 1 large egg
- 3 tablespoons melted butter (slightly cooled) or neutral oil
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract (optional)
- 1½ cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- ½ teaspoon salt

**Method**

1. Add the milk, egg, melted butter and vanilla to the blender.
2. Add the flour, sugar, baking powder and salt.
3. Blend on **low for 5–10 seconds**, until the flour is just mixed in. Scrape down the sides once if needed, then pulse once or twice. **Don't over-blend.**
4. Let the batter **rest for 5 minutes** while the pan heats. It will thicken slightly and bubble.
5. Heat a lightly greased pan or griddle over **medium heat**.
6. Pour about **¼ cup** of batter per pancake straight from the jar.
7. Cook until bubbles form on top and the edges look set (about 2 minutes), then flip and cook for another 1–2 minutes until golden.

**Tip:** If the batter is too thick to pour, add milk a tablespoon at a time and stir it in with a spoon rather than blending again.

## Recipe 2: Thin French crêpes

Makes about 8–10 crêpes.

**Ingredients**

- 1½ cups milk
- 3 large eggs
- 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus extra for the pan
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon sugar (skip for savory crêpes)
- Pinch of salt

**Method**

1. Add the milk, eggs and melted butter to the blender, then the flour, sugar and salt.
2. Blend on **medium for 20–30 seconds** until completely smooth.
3. **Rest the batter for at least 30 minutes** (or refrigerate overnight). This relaxes the gluten and gives thin, tender crêpes that don't tear.
4. Heat a lightly buttered non-stick or crêpe pan over **medium heat**.
5. Pour about **3 tablespoons** of batter into the pan and **swirl immediately** to coat the base in a thin layer.
6. Cook for about 1 minute until the edges lift and the underside is lightly golden, then flip and cook for 20–30 seconds.
7. Stack on a plate and fill with anything you like.

**Filling ideas:** lemon and sugar, chocolate spread and banana, berries and whipped cream (you can [whip cream in your blender](/blog/things-you-can-make-in-a-blender/) too), or ham, cheese and egg for savory crêpes.

## Recipe 3: Banana oat blender pancakes (no flour)

A simple, flour-free option. Use oats labeled gluten-free if needed.

**Ingredients**

- 1 ripe banana
- 2 large eggs
- ½ cup milk (any kind)
- 1 cup rolled oats
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- ½ teaspoon cinnamon
- Pinch of salt

**Method**

1. Add the banana, eggs and milk to the blender, then the oats, baking powder, cinnamon and salt.
2. Blend on **high for 30–45 seconds** until smooth. (Oat batter has no gluten to worry about, so you can blend longer.)
3. Rest for **5–10 minutes** so the oats thicken the batter.
4. Cook small pancakes over **medium-low heat**, since oat pancakes brown quickly and are more delicate to flip.

## Recipe 4: High-protein cottage cheese pancakes

**Ingredients**

- ½ cup cottage cheese
- 2 large eggs
- ¼ cup milk
- ½ cup rolled oats or all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1 teaspoon vanilla
- Pinch of salt

**Method**

1. Blend the cottage cheese, eggs and milk first until completely smooth.
2. Add the oats or flour, baking powder, vanilla and salt, and blend briefly until combined.
3. Cook over medium-low heat.

## Troubleshooting blender pancakes

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tough, rubbery pancakes | Over-blended batter | Blend only until the flour disappears |
| Flat pancakes | Over-mixing, or old baking powder | Blend less; check the baking powder's date |
| Pancakes burn outside, raw inside | Pan too hot | Cook on medium or medium-low |
| Batter too thick to pour | Too much flour, or oat batter thickened | Stir in milk a tablespoon at a time |
| Crêpes tear | Batter didn't rest, or it's too thick | Rest for 30+ minutes; thin with a splash of milk |
| Flour stuck in the corners | Dry ingredients added first | Add liquids first, then dry ingredients |

## Make-ahead tips

- **Pancake batter** made with baking powder is best used within about 30 minutes, as the baking powder starts losing its lift. For make-ahead breakfasts, cook the pancakes and freeze them instead.
- **Crêpe batter** actually improves with rest. Cover and refrigerate it overnight, then stir or pulse briefly before using.
- **Freeze cooked pancakes** in a single layer on a tray, then transfer to a freezer bag. Reheat in a toaster or oven.
- **Batter contains raw eggs and flour**, so don't taste it raw. Keep it refrigerated if you're not cooking right away. Raw flour can carry germs too, not just raw eggs. See [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/) and the [CDC's food safety pages](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/) for general guidance on handling raw eggs and flour.

## Cleaning up

Rinse the jar as soon as you've finished pouring. Dried batter sets like glue. Then use our [60-second self-clean method](/blog/how-to-clean-a-blender/): warm water, a drop of dish soap, and a quick blend.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you make pancake batter in a blender?

Yes. Add the wet ingredients first, then the dry ingredients, and blend on low for 5–15 seconds until the flour just disappears. It's faster than whisking, lump-free, and you can pour straight from the jar.

### Why are my blender pancakes tough?

Over-blending develops gluten in the flour, which makes pancakes tough and flat. Blend only until the flour is mixed in, and let the batter rest for a few minutes before cooking.

### Should crêpe batter rest?

Yes. Resting crêpe batter for at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the fridge, relaxes the gluten and gives thinner, more tender crêpes that are less likely to tear.

### Can I make pancake batter the night before?

Crêpe batter keeps well overnight in the fridge. Pancake batter with baking powder is best used within about 30 minutes, as it gradually loses its rise. Cooking and freezing the pancakes is the better make-ahead option.

### Can I make pancakes with oats instead of flour?

Yes. Blend rolled oats with banana, eggs, milk and baking powder until smooth, rest the batter for a few minutes, then cook over medium-low heat.

## The bottom line

Blender pancake batter is faster, smoother and tidier than whisking by hand. **Load liquids first, blend fluffy pancake batter for just a few seconds, and let crêpe batter blend smooth and rest.** Pour straight from the jar and wash it up right away.
