Making your own baby food sounds like a big project, but with a blender it's one of the simplest things you can do in the kitchen. You cook some vegetables or fruit, blend them with a little liquid, and freeze the extra in portions. That's it.

Homemade purées let you control exactly what goes in (no added salt or sugar), use seasonal produce, and often save money compared with jars and pouches.

This guide covers how to make smooth purées in a blender, how to adjust texture as your baby grows, safe storage, and the foods to avoid.

> **Important:** Every baby is different. **Talk to your pediatrician or health visitor** about when to start solids, which foods to introduce, and how to handle allergens. This guide covers the blender and kitchen side, not medical advice. The American Academy of Pediatrics' parenting site, [HealthyChildren.org](https://www.healthychildren.org/), has reliable guidance on starting solid foods.

## What you need

- **A blender** (countertop, personal or immersion). A high-performance blender gives the smoothest purées, but any blender works with soft, well-cooked food.
- **A steamer basket or saucepan**
- **Ice cube trays or small freezer-safe containers** with lids
- **Clean spoons and a spatula**

## Step 1: Choose your first foods

Common single-ingredient first purées include:

| Vegetables | Fruit |
|---|---|
| Sweet potato | Apple (cooked) |
| Carrot | Pear (cooked) |
| Butternut squash | Banana (ripe, no cooking needed) |
| Peas | Avocado (ripe, no cooking needed) |
| Green beans | Mango (ripe) |
| Zucchini | Peach (cooked or very ripe) |

Many families introduce **one new food at a time** and wait a few days before the next, so it's easier to spot any reactions. Your pediatrician can advise on how to introduce common allergens.

## Step 2: Wash, peel and cook

1. **Wash hands, surfaces and equipment** thoroughly.
2. **Wash, peel and chop** fruit and vegetables into even pieces.
3. **Cook until very soft.** Steaming keeps more flavor and nutrients than boiling. Most vegetables take about 10–20 minutes, depending on the size of the pieces. Pieces should squash easily with a fork.
4. **Let the food cool slightly** before blending. Hot food in a sealed blender can build steam pressure. See our guide to [blending hot food safely](/blog/how-to-blend-hot-soup-safely/).

**No-cook options:** Ripe banana and avocado can be mashed or blended raw.

## Step 3: Blend to the right texture

1. **Add the cooked food to the blender.**
2. **Add a little liquid**: cooking water, breast milk, formula or water, a tablespoon at a time.
3. **Blend until completely smooth** for first foods, scraping down the sides as needed.
4. **Check the texture.** It should be smooth, with no lumps or strings, and loose enough to drip slowly from a spoon.

**Tip:** Fibrous foods like peas and green beans can leave skins. Push the purée through a fine-mesh strainer for extra smoothness if needed.

## Adjusting texture as your baby grows

Texture should gradually progress as your baby develops. Follow your pediatrician's guidance on timing.

| Stage | Texture | Blender tip |
|---|---|---|
| First tastes | Very smooth, thin purée | Blend longer; add more liquid |
| Next | Thicker, smooth purée | Use less liquid |
| Then | Mashed or lumpy textures | Pulse briefly instead of blending, or mash with a fork |
| Later | Soft finger foods | Blender not needed. Cut soft foods into safe pieces |

Moving on from completely smooth purées at the right time helps babies learn to chew and accept new textures.

## Combination ideas

Once individual foods have been introduced, try simple combinations:

- **Sweet potato and apple**
- **Carrot and pear**
- **Butternut squash and peas**
- **Banana and avocado** (no cooking)
- **Peach and mango**
- **Zucchini and pear**

Skip added salt, sugar and honey. Babies don't need them, and honey is unsafe for babies under 12 months (see below).

## Storing and freezing homemade baby food

Homemade purées don't contain preservatives, so store them carefully:

- **Refrigerate promptly** in clean, covered containers. Many food safety guides suggest using refrigerated homemade baby food within **about 1–2 days**.
- **Freeze in portions.** Spoon purée into clean ice cube trays, cover and freeze. Once solid, transfer the cubes to a labeled freezer bag or container. Many sources recommend using frozen purées within about **1–3 months** for best quality.
- **Label everything** with the food and date.
- **Thaw safely** in the refrigerator or by gently warming. Don't thaw at room temperature.
- **Serve from a separate bowl**, not straight from the storage container, and throw away any leftovers from the bowl. Saliva from the spoon can introduce bacteria.
- **When warming, stir well and test the temperature** before serving to avoid hot spots, especially if you use a microwave.

For more detail, see [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/) and the [USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/).

## Foods to avoid or be careful with

- **Honey:** Don't give honey to babies under 12 months old. It can cause infant botulism, even when cooked or baked into foods.
- **Added salt and sugar:** Not needed. Let babies enjoy the natural flavor of foods.
- **Choking hazards:** Whole nuts, whole grapes, popcorn, raw hard vegetables and other small, hard or round foods are choking hazards for young children. When you move on from purées, follow guidance on safe sizes and shapes.
- **Unpasteurized dairy and juices.**
- **Cow's milk as a main drink:** Typically not recommended before 12 months. Ask your pediatrician.

Your pediatrician can give personalized advice, especially on allergens and any family history of allergies.

## Cleaning up

Clean your blender straight after making purées. Dried-on sweet potato and carrot can stain plastic jars. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a regular blender to make baby food?

Yes. Any blender can make baby food if the fruit and vegetables are cooked until very soft. Add a little liquid and scrape down the sides as you blend. High-performance blenders give the smoothest texture.

### How long does homemade baby food last in the fridge?

Many food safety guides suggest using refrigerated homemade baby food within about 1–2 days. Freeze anything you won't use by then.

### How long can I freeze homemade baby food?

For best quality, many sources suggest using frozen purées within about 1–3 months. Label containers with the date.

### Can I add honey to homemade baby food?

No. Honey isn't safe for babies under 12 months old because it can cause infant botulism, even when cooked.

### Should I steam or boil vegetables for baby food?

Steaming is a great option because it keeps more flavor and some nutrients. Boiling works too, and you can use a little of the cooking water to thin the purée.

## The bottom line

Homemade baby food is easy with a blender: **cook until very soft, cool slightly, blend with a little liquid, and freeze extra portions.** Introduce foods one at a time, skip salt, sugar and honey, store purées safely, and move on to thicker textures as your baby grows, guided by your pediatrician.
