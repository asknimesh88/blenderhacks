Protein smoothies are a great breakfast or post-workout snack. They're filling, quick and easy to take with you. But not everyone wants to use protein powder. Some people don't like the taste or texture, some prefer whole foods, and some simply don't want another tub in the cupboard.

The good news: **you can easily get 20 grams of protein or more in a smoothie without any powder**, using everyday ingredients like Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, milk, tofu and seeds.

This guide covers 10 whole-food protein boosters with approximate protein amounts, how to combine them, five high-protein recipes, and tips to keep the texture smooth.

> **About the numbers:** Protein amounts below are **approximate** and vary by brand, product and serving size. Always check the nutrition label on your products. For detailed nutrition data, [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/) is a free, reliable database.

## 10 whole-food protein boosters for smoothies

| Ingredient | Typical amount | Approx. protein | Taste and texture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greek yogurt** (plain) | ¾ cup | 15–20 g | Thick, creamy, tangy |
| **Cottage cheese** | ½ cup | 12–14 g | Creamy when blended; mild |
| **Skyr** (Icelandic yogurt) | ¾ cup | 15–20 g | Very thick, mild |
| **Milk** (dairy) | 1 cup | about 8 g | Neutral |
| **Soy milk** | 1 cup | about 7–8 g | Neutral to slightly nutty |
| **Kefir** | 1 cup | about 9–10 g | Tangy, drinkable |
| **Silken tofu** | ½ cup | about 5–6 g | Completely neutral and silky |
| **Hemp seeds** (hemp hearts) | 3 tbsp | about 9–10 g | Mild, nutty |
| **Peanut butter** | 2 tbsp | about 7–8 g | Rich, nutty |
| **Rolled oats** | ½ cup (dry) | about 5 g | Thick, filling |

**Other helpful additions:** chia seeds (about 4–5 g per 2 tablespoons), almonds or other nuts, pumpkin seeds, and pasteurized liquid egg whites (see the food safety note below).

**Plant milks vary a lot.** Soy and pea milk have much more protein than almond, oat, rice or coconut milk, which often have only about 1–3 grams per cup. Check the label.

## How to reach 20+ grams of protein

The simplest approach is to **combine two or three boosters**. For example:

- **Milk (8 g) + Greek yogurt (15–20 g)** = about **23–28 g**
- **Soy milk (7–8 g) + silken tofu (5–6 g) + hemp seeds (9–10 g)** = about **21–24 g**
- **Milk (8 g) + cottage cheese (12–14 g) + peanut butter (7–8 g)** = about **27–30 g**

**A simple formula:**

1. **A protein-rich liquid:** dairy milk, soy milk or kefir
2. **A creamy protein base:** Greek yogurt, skyr, cottage cheese or silken tofu
3. **A protein topper:** hemp seeds, nut butter, oats or chia seeds
4. **Fruit and flavor:** frozen fruit, cocoa, cinnamon, vanilla

## The best boosters, explained

### Greek yogurt and skyr

The easiest high-protein base. They make smoothies thick and creamy and pair with almost any fruit. Choose **plain** versions to avoid added sugar, and sweeten with fruit instead.

### Cottage cheese

A popular "secret" ingredient. Once blended, cottage cheese becomes completely smooth and creamy, with a mild cheesecake-like flavor. It works especially well with berries, peaches and vanilla. **Blend it with the liquid first** so no curds remain.

### Silken tofu

Silken tofu blends silky smooth with no noticeable flavor, which makes it the best dairy-free protein base. Use **silken** (soft) tofu, not firm tofu, for smoothies.

### Hemp seeds

Hemp hearts are one of the highest-protein seeds, with a mild, nutty flavor. They blend easily, so you don't need a powerful blender.

### Nut butters

Peanut and almond butter add protein, healthy fats and great flavor, especially with chocolate, banana or coffee. You can make your own with our [nut butter guide](/blog/how-to-make-nut-butter-in-a-blender/).

### Oats

Oats add some protein and a lot of staying power. For the smoothest texture, **blend oats with the liquid first** for 20–30 seconds.

## 5 high-protein smoothie recipes (no powder)

Protein amounts are approximate and depend on your ingredients. Follow the [smoothie layering order](/blog/smoothie-layering-order/): liquid first, frozen fruit last.

### 1. Strawberry Cheesecake Protein Smoothie (about 25–30 g)

- 1 cup milk
- ½ cup cottage cheese
- ½ cup Greek yogurt
- 1 cup frozen strawberries
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 teaspoon honey (optional)

Blend the milk and cottage cheese first until completely smooth.

### 2. Peanut Butter Chocolate Protein Shake (about 25–30 g)

- 1 cup milk or soy milk
- ¾ cup Greek yogurt
- 2 tablespoons peanut butter
- 1 tablespoon cocoa powder
- 1 frozen banana (or ½ cup frozen cauliflower rice for less sugar)

Tastes like a milkshake.

### 3. Dairy-Free Berry Tofu Smoothie (about 20–24 g)

- 1 cup soy milk
- ½ cup silken tofu
- 3 tablespoons hemp seeds
- 1 cup frozen mixed berries
- 1 tablespoon maple syrup (optional)

A completely plant-based option.

### 4. Green Protein Smoothie (about 22–26 g)

- 1 cup milk or soy milk
- 1 handful baby spinach
- ¾ cup Greek yogurt
- 1 cup frozen mango
- 1 tablespoon chia seeds
- Squeeze of lime

Blend the milk and spinach first. More tips in our guide to [green smoothies that don't taste like grass](/blog/green-smoothie-that-doesnt-taste-like-grass/).

### 5. Oatmeal Breakfast Protein Smoothie (about 22–26 g)

- 1 cup milk
- ⅓ cup rolled oats
- ½ cup Greek yogurt or skyr
- 1 tablespoon almond butter
- 1 cup frozen blueberries
- Pinch of cinnamon

Blend the milk and oats first for 30 seconds. Very filling.

## Tips for smooth, tasty protein smoothies

- **Blend dense ingredients first.** Cottage cheese, oats, seeds and greens go in with the liquid first for a smooth result.
- **Use frozen fruit instead of ice** for a thick, cold smoothie that isn't watery.
- **Balance the tang.** Greek yogurt and kefir are tangy, so sweet fruits like mango, banana or berries help.
- **Start with less liquid.** Thick bases like yogurt and cottage cheese can make a smoothie very thick, so add liquid gradually.
- **Use a tamper** for very thick shakes. If your blender stalls, see [why your blender won't blend](/blog/blender-not-blending/).

## Common mistakes with high-protein smoothies

**Relying on low-protein plant milk.** Almond, oat, rice and coconut milk usually have much less protein than dairy or soy milk. If you use them, make up the difference with yogurt, tofu, hemp seeds or nut butter.

**Choosing flavored yogurt without checking the label.** Flavored yogurts can contain a lot of added sugar. Plain Greek yogurt or skyr sweetened with fruit gives you more control.

**Not blending cottage cheese long enough.** Visible curds put many people off. Blend cottage cheese with the liquid for 20–30 seconds first, before adding anything else.

**Adding too many thickeners at once.** Greek yogurt, oats, chia and nut butter all thicken. Combine all four and you'll need a spoon. Choose one or two, and add liquid gradually.

**Forgetting flavor balance.** Protein-rich bases like yogurt, cottage cheese and tofu can taste flat or tangy. A ripe banana or mango, vanilla, cinnamon or a pinch of salt makes a big difference.

**Letting it sit.** Smoothies with chia seeds and oats keep thickening in the fridge. If you make one ahead, add an extra splash of liquid and shake or stir before drinking.

## Food safety: raw eggs

Some traditional "protein shake" recipes use raw eggs. Raw eggs carry a risk of salmonella. If you want to add egg, use **pasteurized liquid egg whites** instead, and never use raw eggs for young children, older adults, pregnant people or anyone with a weakened immune system. See [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/) for more.

## How much protein do you need?

Protein needs vary with age, body size, activity level and health. The [Dietary Guidelines for Americans](https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/) and [Nutrition.gov](https://www.nutrition.gov/) have reliable general information. If you have specific health goals, kidney conditions or other medical needs, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I add protein to a smoothie without protein powder?

Use protein-rich whole foods: Greek yogurt, skyr, cottage cheese, dairy or soy milk, kefir, silken tofu, hemp seeds, nut butter, oats and chia seeds. Combining two or three of them easily reaches 20 grams or more.

### Does cottage cheese taste good in smoothies?

Yes. Once blended with the liquid first, it becomes completely smooth, with a mild, slightly cheesecake-like flavor. It pairs especially well with berries and vanilla.

### What is the best dairy-free protein for smoothies?

Silken tofu, soy milk and hemp seeds are excellent dairy-free options. Soy milk has much more protein than most other plant milks, and silken tofu blends completely smooth with no flavor.

### Can I put raw eggs in a smoothie for protein?

It's not recommended because of the salmonella risk. Use pasteurized liquid egg whites instead, or choose other protein sources like yogurt or tofu.

### How much protein is in a smoothie with Greek yogurt?

It depends on the brand and amount, but plain Greek yogurt typically provides roughly 15–20 grams per ¾ cup. Combined with a cup of dairy milk, a smoothie can easily reach 20–28 grams.

## The bottom line

A high-protein smoothie doesn't need a scoop of powder. **Combine a protein-rich liquid with a creamy protein base like Greek yogurt, cottage cheese or silken tofu, and top it with seeds or nut butter.** You'll easily reach 20 grams or more with whole foods you probably already have.
