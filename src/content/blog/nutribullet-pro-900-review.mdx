If you want one smoothie a day with as little fuss as possible, a personal blender like the NutriBullet Pro 900 makes a lot of sense. You fill the cup, twist it onto the base, blend, and drink from the same cup.

> **How we reviewed this:** We haven't tested the Pro 900 ourselves. This review is based on the manufacturer's specifications, the warranty terms, and long-term feedback from owners. See [how we score](/about/#how-we-score).

## Who it's for

- People who make **one smoothie or protein shake at a time**
- Small kitchens, dorms and offices where counter space is tight
- Anyone who hates cleaning a big blender jar

## Who should skip it

If you regularly blend for a family, make nut butters, or want to blend hot soup, a personal blender is the wrong tool. Look at a full-size or high-performance blender instead. Our guide to [blender types](/blog/blender-types-explained/) explains the difference.

<PullQuote>A personal blender is the right choice when convenience matters more than capacity.</PullQuote>

## Getting the best results

Personal blenders are flipped upside down to blend, so the loading order is different from a countertop blender. Check the manual, keep below the max line, and see our tips on [smoothie layering](/blog/smoothie-layering-order/).
